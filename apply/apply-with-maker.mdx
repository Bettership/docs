Maker helps carry what you're learning into the AI tools you use for your work.

## What you can make

- A **prompt** gives an AI tool reusable instructions for a particular job.
- A **context pack** gives it relevant background about you, your knowledge, or your approach.
- A **skill** packages a method with instructions and references, such as a way to critique interview questions against what your resources teach.

Example ask: “Use these interview guides to make a prompt that helps me spot leading questions. I'll use it in ChatGPT.”

Tell Maker what the result should help you do, where you'll use it, and which resources to draw from. Maker can read the shared workspace to fill in relevant context.

## From a draft to something useful

For a skill, Maker proposes what to make and waits for your agreement. A direct request for a prompt can go straight into production.

The result has saved files, the resources it drew on, and instructions for using it. You can ask for revisions, with earlier versions preserved. Review both the result and any demo before relying on it.

## Where to find Maker

Open **Apply → Maker** for a conversation. The **Studio** holds Maker's drafts alongside items you've already made. A draft is an idea for something to make, not a finished item.

## What Maker cannot do yet

Maker cannot automatically install or sync items into your other tools. You download or copy the files and follow the use instructions.

Items don't refresh on a schedule. You request an update or revision. Maker doesn't yet produce deployed agent bundles.

Learning materials, such as study guides and cheat sheets, belong with Coach. Maker's main job is reusable tools that help you apply what you've learned.
